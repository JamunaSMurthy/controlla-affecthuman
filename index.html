Controlla: Learning Controllability via Graph-Constrained Latent Geometry

Jamuna S. Murthy1, Amin Karimi Monsefi2, Rajiv Ramnath2
1MS Ramaiah Institute of Technology, 2The Ohio State University
Paper PDF Code Data

Controlla treats controllability as structured latent geometry for identity-preserving multimodal affective control.

Abstract

Controllable multimodal generation is commonly formulated as an inference-time conditioning problem using prompts, guidance, or auxiliary modules. While effective, such approaches do not explicitly structure how semantic attributes evolve, which can lead to identity drift and inconsistent cross-modal behavior.

We propose Controlla, a modular factorized-control framework that treats controllability as a property of structured latent geometry. Controlla learns identity and attribute factors from multimodal inputs and aligns them with graph priors using graph-constrained optimal transport, encouraging attributes to follow graph-consistent trajectories while preserving reference identity.

To evaluate this setting, we construct AffectHuman-43K, a leakage-aware multimodal benchmark for reference-grounded affective control, and introduce geometry-aware metrics for trajectory consistency and latent disentanglement. Experiments show consistent improvements in controllability, identity preservation, and cross-modal alignment.

Identity-Preserving Edits

Controlla maps image, reference image, text, and audio into factorized identity and attribute spaces, then edits affective attributes while keeping the reference-grounded identity stable.

Affective Control

Emotion and identity graph priors are aligned with learned factors using graph-constrained optimal transport, encouraging consistent semantic traversal instead of unconstrained edits.

Latent Control

Graph-consistent traversal

Semantic edits are modeled as structured latent transformations. Fused Gromov-Wasserstein alignment organizes the attribute space around affective graph priors, while identity alignment stabilizes the reference-identity space.

Graph-guided transitions

Graph-guided traversal produces smoother, more interpretable emotion transitions than linear interpolation while keeping identity consistent across the path.

AffectHuman-43K

AffectHuman-43K is a leakage-aware benchmark designed for emotion-aligned controlled evaluation. Identity is visually specified by a reference image, while text and audio provide affective control signals.

Results

Controlla improves emotion accuracy, trajectory smoothness, and human preference on AffectHuman-43K, while geometry-aware evaluation shows stronger latent disentanglement and graph consistency with competitive identity preservation.
