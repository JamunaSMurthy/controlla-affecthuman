Overview

Controlla studies controllable generation as a problem of structured latent geometry rather than only inference-time conditioning. Given a reference identity and multimodal affective controls such as text and audio, the goal is to modify affective attributes while preserving the reference identity.

Key Idea

Identity Preservation

Uses a reference image anchor to preserve identity while affective attributes are edited.

Multimodal Control

Combines visual, textual, and audio affective signals for controlled generation.

Latent Geometry

Models controllability through structured latent traversal and graph-consistent paths.

Benchmark

Introduces AffectHuman-43K for reference-grounded affective controllability evaluation.

AffectHuman-43K Benchmark

AffectHuman-43K is a controlled multimodal benchmark for evaluating reference-grounded, identity-preserving affective control. The benchmark is designed to test whether a model can modify affective attributes while preserving the identity specified by a reference image.

42K+ Samples

Image, reference image, text, and audio coverage for controlled evaluation.

Identity-Disjoint Splits

Reference identity groups are separated across train, validation, and test splits.

Eight Emotions

Includes happiness, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, disgust, contempt, and neutral.

Method Summary

  • Decomposes latent space into identity-related and affective attribute-related components.
  • Uses graph-constrained optimal transport to structure affective transitions.
  • Encourages smooth, graph-consistent trajectories between affective states.
  • Evaluates controllability using accuracy, trajectory smoothness, identity preservation, latent disentanglement, and geodesic consistency.
